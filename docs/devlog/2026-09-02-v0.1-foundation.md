# Devlog [2026-09-02]: v0.1 Foundation & Citation Graph Architecture

## 1. Context & Problem
Traditional keyword-based search engine models treat scientific literature as isolated documents. To enable structural analysis, bibliographic metadata must be transformed into a directed knowledge graph.

## 2. Architectural Decisions & Trade-offs
- **Snowball Sampling Strategy:** Rather than pulling random disconnected works from OpenAlex, we implemented seed sampling (2,000 top-cited works) followed by 1-hop reference expansion to guarantee graph density.
- **Closed-Graph Constraint:** Pruned dangling edges that point outside the retrieved corpus to prevent distortion in PageRank calculations.
- **In-Memory GDS Projection:** Offloaded PageRank and Louvain Community Detection to Neo4j Graph Data Science (GDS) to allow parallelized graph processing.

## 3. Preliminary Findings
- Standard citation counts can be misleading: foundational reference databases (e.g., Pfam, SCOP) exhibit structurally high PageRank scores despite moderate raw citation counts in subset windows.
- Louvain modularity indicates distinct thematic research clusters emerging organically from citation pathways without explicit ontological labels.

## 4. Next Steps (v0.2)
- Integrate interactive sub-graph visualizations (e.g., PyVis / Streamlit AgGraph).
- Experiment with Leiden algorithm for improved community partition stability.
